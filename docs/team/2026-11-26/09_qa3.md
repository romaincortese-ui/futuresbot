## 1. Headline

**The trading code is careful; the operation around it is fragile.** Every serious incident came from outside the
strategy: a credential, a laptop, a login, a stake below the exchange minimum. Nothing watches the bot from outside, and
the $140 floor is only an alert from a laptop that has been down since about 21 October.

## 3. What failed silently

1. **No outside watchdog.** Alerts come only from the bot (Telegram) and the laptop audit. No health check in
   `railway.toml`, no dead-man switch: if the bot hangs or the laptop is off, nobody is told.
2. **Safety rules live in documents, not code.** The $140 floor needs a human to read an alert; that chain broke on 10-21.
3. **Exchange refusals look like bugs.** 7008 arrives as a runtime error, so a starved trial looks like a broken bot.
4. **Retention clocks.** MEXC keeps history ~90 days and 1m bars ~30 days; beyond that only our archive has it.

## 4. What any new or continued project must design out

1. **Single-laptop, single-login dependence.** Monitoring and evidence capture run server-side with read-only credentials;
   data stored off the laptop.
2. **Credential expiry.** Every key gets a recorded expiry date and a 7/2/0-day alert, or a fixed IP so it does not expire.
3. **Advisory-only kill rules.** Floor and drawdown rules pause entries automatically; only the owner resumes.
4. **Exchange minimums.** Check the minimum order before sending; record "too small" as a clean skip, not an error.
5. **Silent data loss.** Daily reconciliation of our ledger against the venue, and archives kept for at least a year.
6. **A venue we may legally use.** MEXC lists the UK as a prohibited jurisdiction, and the FCA bans crypto derivatives for
   UK retail (Architect memo §b, citing mexc.com/terms and fca.org.uk). An account that can be frozen is an operational
   risk no code can fix.

## 5. Go-live acceptance criteria (all must be true before real money is used again)

**Legal and venue**
- A1. The venue and product are legal for the owner, confirmed in writing; funds are recoverable.
- A2. Starting stake is above the venue minimums for at least 95% of the orders the strategy would send (from a replay).

**Edge evidence**
- A3. The strategy passed its pre-registered test on data it never saw (the locked holdout), after fees and funding.
- A4. At least 2 weeks of paper or tiny-size live running where replayed and real fills match within agreed limits.

**Safety automation (tested, not just written)**
- A5. Equity floor and max-drawdown pause act automatically inside the bot, with tests that prove a pause happens.
- A6. Every open position has an exchange-side stop at all times, verified after restarts.
- A7. Key expiry alert configured and shown on the boot log; a test alert reaches the owner's phone.

**Monitoring independent of the bot**
- A8. An outside watchdog alerts the owner within 15 minutes if the bot stops sending heartbeats.
- A9. The daily audit runs server-side, not on a laptop, and a missed audit raises an alert by itself.
- A10. Evidence (trades, ledger, market bars) is archived daily to durable storage with 12+ months retention.

**Drills (each run once and logged)**
- A11. Key expired, Telegram down, restart with a position open, exchange refuses an order: each produces the right alert
  and a safe state.
- A12. The owner can pause, check equity and close everything from a phone in under 5 minutes, while travelling.

Today the bot meets roughly A6 and partly A7. It fails A1, A3, A5, A8, A9, A10 and A2 (at $35).

## My position for the meeting

**Pivot to a new project**, carrying over the good parts (exchange-side stops, replay harness, pre-registration habit).
The current bot cannot pass acceptance: its venue is not legal for a UK resident and its safety net depends on a laptop.
Fixing those is a rebuild of the operation anyway, so build it once, for an edge that has passed its holdout first.
