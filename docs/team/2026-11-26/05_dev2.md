# Developer 2 (integrations) prep memo — 2026-11-26

Summary: **every venue we could legally use from the UK is a spot or wrapper venue, not a leveraged crypto venue.** For a small account, Interactive Brokers UK is the strongest single integration target. Kraken or Coinbase are the options if we keep a spot-crypto sleeve. Our incident log already tells us how to build the plumbing properly.

**Data and sandboxes**
- **Free:** public market data endpoints on Kraken, Coinbase and MEXC (bars, funding rates). This is all the futures lab needs.
- **CoinGlass API** (funding, open interest, liquidations across exchanges): from $29/month, 30 requests/min, personal use only [16].
- **ETF/ETN history:** IBKR market data (needs subscriptions, cost unknown). Free long daily history sources are still to be chosen.
- **Paper trading:** IBKR paper, IG demo, Saxo SIM and Trading 212 practice mode all let us run a new tool end to end before real money goes in.

**Small capital:** at £3 per trade, rebalancing 6 ETFs every month on £5k costs about 4% a year. Rebalancing quarterly costs about 1.4% a year. **We need about £5-10k or more, and trades only a few times a year.**

## 2. Integration best practices learnt from our incidents

1. **Key expiry (MEXC, 2026-10-14).** The key died at 15:31Z. The bot logged more than 830 failed calls and could not close anything. Only the resting exchange stops protected us (`docs/daily_audit.md`, around line 404). *Do:* record every key's expiry date when it is created. Alert 14, 7 and 1 days before. Check a cheap authenticated call every tick and **alert on the first auth failure**, not after a streak. Always keep stops resting on the venue.
2. **Never substitute numbers for unreadable data.** After the auth failure the bot printed `equity=77.85`, which was most likely a fallback figure rather than a real reading (same audit entry). *Do:* treat an unreadable value as "unknown", stop trading, and alert.
3. **Single-point-of-failure credentials.** Railway OAuth on one laptop expired around 10-21, and the audit has been blind for five weeks. A full laptop disk also killed the evidence archive (`00_briefing_pack.md` §4). *Do:* run the audit on a cloud scheduler with its own **read-only, trade-disabled, withdrawal-disabled, IP-restricted** key. Add a dead-man's-switch alert when no audit has run in 26 hours.
4. **Idempotent orders.** We already fixed this. Each order is sent once with a unique `externalOid` and, on timeout or a 5xx error, looked up by that ID instead of re-sent (`futuresbot/marketdata.py` lines 26-44). Keep this pattern on any new venue. IBKR, Kraken and Coinbase support client order IDs. Trading 212 does not, so we would need to check our open orders before any retry.
5. **Pre-trade checks against venue rules.** At $35, error 7008 ($5 minimum) starved trial 27. *Do:* load each venue's minimums, lot sizes and tick sizes at start-up, and reject or resize **before** sending. Choose a venue whose minimums suit our capital.
6. **Own the ledger.** MEXC keeps about 90 days of positions and about 30 days of 1-minute bars. Write every fill, fee and funding payment to our own durable store each day. The daily reconcile ("N exchange rows = N store rows") worked well and caught a manual SOL trade nobody had recorded (`daily_audit.md` around line 10867). Keep it, and alert on any mismatch, any orphan, or a position with no stop (`docs/DECISION_RULE.md` around line 4674).
7. **Secrets and sessions.** Keep keys in the platform's secret store, use separate trade and read keys, rotate them on a calendar, and log only a key's prefix. IBKR gateway sessions also need periodic re-login (from developer experience; check the IBKR docs), so plan a health check and a human re-authentication path from day one.
