## 2. Did 27 trials teach us about an edge, or mostly noise?

**The trial P&L numbers are noise.** Every trial's standard error was bigger than its total (e.g. `daily_audit.md:290`, `:161`). The biggest single-configuration sample is 66 closes (trial 25). My rough arithmetic: with each trade swinging about +/-1R, proving even a +0.1R edge needs roughly 400 trades on one unchanged setting. We never got past a sixth of that. About 30 configurations ran in ~95 days, about one every 3 days. Trials 25-27 never got near their pre-registered 250-close decision point; only 18 and 19F reached even their 30-close bars.

**The research produced real negative knowledge**, and that has value:
- Thin stops are eaten by fees. This came from 316 fires (`DECISION_RULE_trial3_archived.md`). Fees and minimum order sizes dominate at small stakes.
- The entry signals carry about zero edge. A coin-flip side scored within noise of the signals' own side (`DECISION_RULE.md:19372-19384`), and "every WILDCARD setup loses on the unseen year" (`:19405`).
- Exit tweaks cannot rescue a strategy whose entries have no edge. Fee trail, tight loss cut, peak exits and ratchets were all refuted (`:19319-19357`). The file has 68 REJECTED or REFUTED headings.
- TREND losses come from the market moving during the trade, and market state at entry does not predict that (`:19576-19589`).
- Today's edge study found no 80-90% rule. Its best cells were matched by shuffled data, which leaves three unproven leads (briefing s.5).

**Plain verdict:** strong evidence that no *large* edge exists in this design, and no evidence for any edge.

## 3. Process failures

1. **Trial churn.** We changed the setting before any trial could be read. The log warned about this on 08-10 ("A trial that never runs long enough to score cannot be wrong, and cannot be right either", `DECISION_RULE.md:4788`), and the pattern continued through trial 27.
2. **We added money after a small win.** Trial 18 "passed" on 27 trades. The account was then funded about 5x and lost $198 in three weeks. The same thing happened to TREND: 7 wins out of 7 in trial 15 (`:5889`), then -0.45R per trade in trial 25, then it was switched off.
3. **Cash moves wrecked trials.** Withdrawals mid-trial reset the trial segments. The $150 withdrawal starved trial 27 below the $5 minimum (`DECISION_RULE.md:19781-19787`).
4. **Operations were fragile.** For trial 2 the config was not actually live for 70 hours (`DECISION_RULE_trial3_archived.md`). The MEXC API key expired on 10-14 (`daily_audit.md:404`). The laptop disk filled and the Railway login expired around 10-21, so the audit has been dark for 5 weeks (`daily_audit.md:116-122`). MEXC deletes data after 30-90 days, so that evidence is being lost.
5. **Analysis overhead and corrections.** The decision file is 19,787 lines. The value of 1R was "corrected" several times ($4.09 / $15.49 / $25.16, `:1162`, `:1871`). The owner cannot realistically read the record.
6. **We checked the legal basis late.** MEXC lists the UK as a Prohibited Jurisdiction, and MEXC Global has been on the FCA Warning List since 2024-03-22. This was first recorded on 10-06, almost six months in (`DECISION_RULE.md:19556`).
7. **We optimised a tiny account.** By our own arithmetic, even a working edge was worth -$11 to +$22 a month at this size (`DECISION_RULE.md:1-25`). Every hour of effort chased a few dollars.

## 4. Lessons learnt

**Keep doing**
- Pre-register with a hash before any flag changes. Keep the "do not re-propose without new evidence" list.
- Lock a holdout, and test every rule against a luck benchmark (shuffled outcomes).
- Use independent verifiers and replay-parity checks before we trust a simulator.
- Reconcile against the exchange read-only, and always compare with a passive benchmark (T-bill, index, BTC hold).

**Stop doing**
- Switching trials before their decision point. Treat one config for 60+ days, or a fixed n, as untouchable.
- Adding money on fewer than ~100 trades, or on any in-sample result.
- Spending effort on exit tweaks while the entries show no edge.
- Using live real money to learn what an offline replay can tell us for free.
- Running critical ops from a personal laptop, and writing logs nobody can read.
- Trading on a venue that excludes the owner's jurisdiction.
