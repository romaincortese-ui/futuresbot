## 2. What is wrong or weak

1. **The briefing table leaves out the worst month.** From 08-29 to 09-25, 116 closed trades lost **−$180.20**, and the week of 09-07 alone lost −$168. The full 90-day total is **−$185.57** over 276 trades, profit factor 0.74. "−$5.38 over 2 months" looks like breakeven only because position size fell after the $715 withdrawal.
2. **Small split error.** The 10-21 boundary is 125/35 trades, not 126/34. One BR_USDT trade closed at 02:00 UTC on 10-21. The 10-21 to 11-26 total is +$0.13, not $0.00. This does not change any conclusion.
3. **"Shorts made money" depends on the period.** In the 241 discovery trades, shorts lost −$58.89 (profit factor 0.62). The old file also used leverage up to x100 (10 trades at x50/x100), not x5–x25 as the briefing says.
4. **Some features measure shorter windows than their names say.** In `build_features.py`, `tok_ret_Nh` covers N−1 to N hours, so `tok_ret_1h` is really "since the last hourly close". `btc_ret_Nh` covers **N−2 hours**: lead 2's "BTC 24h" is a 22-hour return, and `btc_ret_4h` is a 2-hour return. This happens because the code subtracts 1 hour from the window and also uses the last finished close. It is not look-ahead, but any live filter must copy these definitions exactly.
5. **None of the three leads survives a multiple-testing correction.** The report's own luck benchmark says the best of 36 inputs reaches a ≥27% spread by chance 5% of the time. Lead 1's spread is 25%, so it does not clear that bar. The printed p=0.004 is for the input chosen *because* it ranked best, so it is not valid. I reran the test with 500 shuffles, once with independent shuffles and once with time-preserving shifts. The family-wise p-value is **≈0.15–0.17** for lead 1 and ≈0.96 for lead 2 (lead 2's own p is 0.09–0.12). Lead 3's spread (12%) is below the typical luck spread (21%). The only support for any lead is that it looked similar in both halves of the data, and that is weak evidence.
6. **Lead 1 is the wrong story.** It is the *middle* third of the 10-hour move (3.9–11.7%). The top third (>12.5% already moved in the trade's direction) won 53% and made +1.25% per trade. "Don't chase" does not describe that pattern. A middle-only effect like this is a typical result of searching through many cuts of the data.
7. **Win rate and dollars point in different directions.** Discovery trades average **+0.50% net per trade** (each trade counted equally, before leverage), yet they lost −$183. The 20 worst trades total −$403, and all were entered 6–14 September at larger size. Trades entered before 09-26 lost −$180; trades entered after lost −$3. Leads 1+2 "fix" the result in hindsight (96 trades kept at +$84.79, −$267.94 removed) mostly by removing that fortnight. The loss is a sizing and regime problem more than an entry problem.
8. **The trades are not independent.** The 235 trades fall on only 50 days, up to 12 per day. Lead 2's bucket covers 32 days. Shuffle tests and Wilson intervals treat every trade as independent, so they overstate confidence.
9. **The data mixes several configurations.** Discovery spans several trials with different leverage and sizing, and the tercile cut-points were set on the whole set before the halves check.
10. **The "+28.1R" shadow-ledger claim is thin.** It rests on 47 "slot_occupied" rows marked "directional paper only" (`docs/daily_audit.md` L179, L257, L314). Whether fees and slippage are included is unknown. It should not be used as evidence that the real trades are a biased sample.

## 3. Test protocol Approach 2 must pass

- **P0 Pre-register (frozen, hashed file, before any run).** Exact feature code, the lead thresholds (no re-tuning), the one exit rule, fees (`feeRates` 0.06–0.08% per side for alts), slippage, funding, the $5 minimum order, one primary metric (mean net R per trade after costs), and the pass bar.
- **P1 Feature unit tests.** Synthetic bars with known returns: assert that a 24h feature spans 24h. Truncation test: cut all data at each entry time, recompute, and require identical features.
- **P2 Replay fidelity.** Replay the 235 discovery trades from their real entries with the live exit logic. Require ≥90% agreement on win/loss and a total within ±20% of real dollars. Report misses individually. If it fails, stop.
- **P3 Universe and survivorship.** Count candidates that have no bars (delisted coins). Score them at −1R (worst case) and show that results survive.
- **P4 Fills.** Enter at the next bar open plus slippage. No fills on the signal bar. Candidates under the minimum order or blocked by full slots are tagged and reported separately.
- **P5 Statistics.** Log every rule tried and correct for all of them (family-wise or Bonferroni). Use a day-block bootstrap; the 95% CI of mean net R must sit above 0. The result must beat both "take every candidate" and random entries (random time, same coin and side).
- **P6 Out-of-sample.** Walk forward through time, then make **one** pre-registered look at the holdout. The 35 holdout trades are too few: telling 66% wins apart from 50% needs about 60–75 independent trades (alpha 0.05, power 0.8), and more because trades cluster by day. Fix the required n before looking.
- **P7 Money test.** Simulate at the live stake, in dollars after all costs, with maximum drawdown. Compare with a cash or index alternative over the same period.
